  <h2>1. The After-Hours Shockwave: When Momentum Meets Valuation Gravity</h2>
  <p class="direct-answer-chunk" style="font-size:16px; line-height:1.6; font-weight:600; color:var(--text-primary); background:var(--bg-card-hover); padding:12px 16px; border-left:3px solid #0284c7; border:1px solid var(--border-subtle); border-radius:6px; margin-bottom:16px;">
    The sudden $500B paper contraction across US semiconductor megacaps wasn't an isolated technical glitch. It marked the precise inflection point where tier-one Wall Street algorithms identified that forward capital expenditures by hyperscalers were outrunning visible enterprise monetization.
  </p>
  <p>
    For the past 24 months, the global financial landscape operated under a singular narrative: buy artificial intelligence hardware at any valuation. Semiconductor titans experienced unprecedented multiple expansion, trading at forward price-to-earnings multiples exceeding 45x while discounting flawless, uninterrupted quarter-over-quarter exponential growth.
  </p>
  <p>
    However, beneath the headline frenzy, a harsh physical and financial reality began surfacing. The next generation of 100,000-GPU clusters requires multi-gigawatt electrical substations, nuclear reactor power purchase agreements, and transmission line approvals that carry multi-year regulatory lead times. When major institutional models adjusted for these physical deployment bottlenecks, forward free cash flow estimates underwent sudden downward revisions.
  </p>

  <!-- SECTION 2: Understanding Capital Rotation -->
  <h2>2. The Mechanism of Institutional Capital Rotation: Where Did the Money Go?</h2>
  <p>
    When foreign institutional investors (FIIs) manage trillions in sovereign pension assets, they rarely move to 100% cash. Cash incurs drag against global inflation and benchmark hurdle rates. Instead, institutional risk-parity desks initiate what portfolio managers term a <strong>"sector and sovereign rotation"</strong>:
  </p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Harvesting High-Multiple Beta:</strong> Capital is trimmed from over-owned, crowded trades where upside surprises are fully priced in.</li>
    <li><strong>Allocating Into Real Yields:</strong> Liquidity is systematically re-routed into emerging market equities demonstrating sovereign macroeconomic stability, robust domestic capital formation, and defensive balance sheets.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>
    Over the trailing trading cycles, foreign institutional desks orchestrated an estimated <strong>₹65,700 Crore reallocation</strong> into Indian equities. While everyday social media traders panicked over intraday red candles in tech stocks, institutional custody accounts were absorbing large tranches of Indian private sector banks, infrastructure conglomerates, and transmission utilities.
  </p>

  <!-- SECTION 4: The Domestic SIP Cushion -->
  <h2>4. The Domestic Fortress: Why Foreign Selling No Longer Breaks Indian Markets</h2>
  <p>
    In previous market cycles—such as 2008 or 2013—a global institutional liquidation would trigger an indiscriminate 20% to 30% collapse in the benchmark Nifty 50. Today, the fundamental market architecture of Dalal Street has been permanently transformed by the domestic retail investor.
  </p>
  <p>
    Monthly systematic investment plans (SIPs) from salaried Indian households now channel over <strong>₹25,000 Crore every single month</strong> directly into domestic mutual funds. This perpetual domestic bid creates an unyielding structural liquidity floor. When global funds sell into short-term panics, domestic asset management companies (AMCs) step in as net buyers, absorbing supply at attractive valuations.
  </p>
