The Macro Thesis: Zudio's Working Capital Velocity Engine</h2> <p> Trent's explosive market capitalization expansion—growing from under ₹7,000 Crore in FY18 to over ₹2,40,000 Crore—is primarily the story of <strong>Zudio's inventory turnover velocity</strong>. Traditional departmental fashion retailers like Shoppers Stop operate on 120 to 140 inventory days with gross margins of 45-52%. In contrast, Zudio operates on an aggressive, value-first proposition: </p> <ul> <li><strong>Price Architecture:</strong> Over 82% of all merchandise priced below ₹499; 100% of SKUs below ₹999.</li> <li><strong>Inventory Velocity:</strong> 70 to 75 inventory days, enabling 4.8 to 5.2 full inventory turns annually.</li> <li><strong>Store Expansion Velocity:</strong> Rollout cadence exceeding 180 to 220 new stores every twelve months across Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities in India.</li> </ul> <p> Because Zudio relies on leased commercial footprints rather than outright real estate ownership, its financial statements are heavily reshaped by the mandatory implementation of <strong>IndAS-116 (Leases)</strong>, effective since FY20. </p> <h2>2. IndAS-116 Forensic Deconstruction: ROU Assets & Capitalized Leases</h2> <p> Under pre-IndAS accounting, rental payments for retail stores were treated entirely as operating expenses within the Profit & Loss statement. Under IndAS-116: </p> <ol> <li>The discounted present value of all future lease commitments is capitalized on the asset side as a <strong>Right of Use (ROU) Asset</strong>.</li> <li>An equal and corresponding obligation is recognized on the liabilities side as a <strong>Lease Liability</strong> (split into current and non-current financial obligations).</li> <li>Rental expenses are completely removed from EBITDA. Instead, the P&L reflects <strong>Depreciation of the ROU Asset</strong> and a <strong>Finance Charge (Interest) on the Lease Liability</strong>.</li> </ol> <p> This accounting standard creates an optical surge in reported EBITDA and EBIT margins, which retail investors frequently misinterpret as genuine operational pricing power. </p> <h3>Table 1: Trent Ltd 10-Year Audited Financial Statements & ROCE Deconstruction (FY16 – FY25 TTM)</h3> <div style="overflow-x:auto; The Debt-to-Equity Reality: Financial Debt vs. Lease Obligations</h2> <p> If an analyst examines only Trent's bank borrowings and commercial paper, the company appears virtually net-cash positive, carrying modest short-term borrowings under ₹500 Crore. </p> <p> However, retail store leases are <strong>non-cancellable contract obligations</strong> with standard lock-in clauses of 3 to 9 years. When lease liabilities of <strong>₹6,750 Crore</strong> are factored into capital structure calculations, Trent's economic Debt-to-Equity ratio climbs to <strong>1.85x</strong>. In an economic downturn where same-store sales decline by even 4% to 6%, fixed lease depreciation continues relentlessly, creating severe operating deleverage. </p> <h2>4. DuPont ROE Decomposition: How Trent Compounds</h2> <p> Decomposing Trent's Return on Equity ($ROE = ext{Net Margin} imes ext{Asset Turnover} imes ext{Financial Leverage}$) illustrates that the expansion in ROE from 6% to over 28% was not generated by widening net margins (which remain modest at 8.5% to 9.2%). Instead, it was catalyzed by a dramatic surge in <strong>Fixed Asset Turnover (inventory velocity)</strong> combined with the structural financial leverage inherent in IndAS-116 lease commitments. </p> <h2>5.
