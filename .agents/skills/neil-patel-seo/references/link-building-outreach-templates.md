# The Neil Patel Link-Building & Outreach Machine

This manual provides the exact outreach email frameworks, pitch structures, and follow-up sequences from Neil Patel’s **SEO Unlocked** program that convert at 12% to 22% response rates.

---

## The Golden Rules of Neil Patel Outreach

1. **Keep it under 100 words**: Editors and webmasters receive 50+ pitches daily. If your pitch requires scrolling, it gets deleted.
2. **Personalize the opening**: Mention a specific recent article they published. Prove you are a human who actually read their work.
3. **Never ask for a link in Email #1**: Ask for permission or feedback first. Once they say *"Sure, send it over"*, you have conversational commitment.
4. **Give Before You Ask**: Point out a broken link or a typo on their site as a genuine favor before introducing your resource.
5. **Always provide pre-formatted embed code**: Make it effortless for them to publish your visual asset.

## 1. Infographic & Visual Asset Syndication Template

### Step 1: Initial Hook & Permission
**Subject:** Quick question about your [Topic] piece on [Site Name]

```
Hey [First Name],

I was just reading your recent piece on [Topic] and loved your breakdown of [Specific Point]. 

My research desk just published a visual data infographic deconstructing [Core Topic / Surprising Finding]:
[Link to Infographic]

Thought your readers might find it useful as an visual supplement. If you'd like to feature it, I'd be happy to write a bespoke 200-word unique introductory paragraph specifically tailored for your blog.

Let me know if you'd like me to send over the embed snippet!

Best,
[Your Name]
[Your Role, The Bull Print]
```

### Step 2: The Follow-Up with Embed Snippet
```
Hey [First Name],

Here is the embed code with attribution:

<p><strong>Infographic courtesy of <a href="[Your Canonical URL]">[Your Brand Name]</a>:</strong></p>
<p><a href="[Your Canonical URL]"><img src="[Your WebP Image URL]" alt="[Alt Description]" width="800" border="0" /></a></p>

Let me know if you need any custom dimensions or styling!
```

## 2. Broken Link Building ("The Helpful Fixer")

**Subject:** Broken link on your [Page Title] resource page

```
Hey [First Name],

I was browsing your excellent resource list on [Page Topic] ([URL of their page]) and noticed that one of the links to [Old Resource Name] seems to be broken and returning a 404 error.

We recently published an updated, 2026 comprehensive guide on that exact topic, complete with fresh data tables and free calculators:
[Your Guide URL]

It might make a great replacement for your readers so they don't hit that dead link.

Either way, thanks for putting together such a great resource!

Cheers,
[Your Name]
```

## 3. The "Moving Man" Technique (Rebranded / Closed Competitor)

**Subject:** Outdated link to [Dead/Rebranded Service] on [Site Name]

```
Hey [First Name],

I noticed you mentioned [Old/Defunct Tool or Service] in your article on [Topic] ([URL of their post]).

Just an FYI—[Old Service] shut down / discontinued that feature last quarter and their landing page is now dead.

If you're looking for an active, updated alternative to recommend to your audience, our team built an interactive tool that handles [Specific Function]:
[Your Tool URL]

Hope this helps you keep your article up to date!

Warmly,
[Your Name]
```
